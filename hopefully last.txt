

"69th in line for a throne that no longer exists, heir to a kingdom buried in ash, and absolutely willing to introduce himself at length before caving your skull in."

Origins — The Branch of Rochefort

Born into the branch family of the Rochefort lineage, Henri Hector Augustin de la Rochefort-Lumière stood 69th in line to inherit the seat of the Protector of the Lumière Kingdom — a number distant enough to mean a life of comfort without responsibility, and close enough to carry the full weight of the name. It was, by all accounts, a happy life. Heaven mandated nothing less for those of royal blood. Then Henri turned five, and the neighbouring empire disagreed.

The Fall — Fire, Ash & a Merciful Sword

The kingdom was obliterated. Henri fled with a minor wound and a terror too large for a five-year-old to comprehend. The soldier sent to finish the job — Ervid Korr, a seasoned warrior of the invading army — found him alone in the rubble and could not bring himself to kill a child in cold blood. Instead, he gave the boy shelter, then went further still: he took Henri with him and fled the continent entirely, leaving behind rank, duty, and whatever conscience had driven him to war in the first place. They arrived together in the city of Aretrea, each carrying the weight of a ruined world.

What the Disaster Left Behind

The rage

Smouldering embers of a deep-seated fury, banked low but never extinguished.

The name

All that remains of the Rochefort-Lumière line, and he will not let anyone forget it.

The walking stick

A habit inherited from his father. The one piece of the old world he carried out alive.

Aretrea — The Making of a Warrior

Ervid joined the city guard. Henri, aged five and already furious about it, demanded to be trained. He never wanted to be helpless again. Ervid obliged — years of weapons drills, basic combat, and gruelling strength work that the boy threw himself into with a ferocity that surprised even his mentor. A mantra

"They can't attack you if they're too busy defending themselves."

The greataxe

On his 15th birthday, Ervid gifted him a walking stick that unfolds into a greataxe. Henri wept. Then immediately broke a training dummy with it.

Aversion to heavy armour

Feels like a cage. He would rather bleed freely than be slowed down. Scale mail is already a compromise.

The Caravan Years — Desert Work

At fifteen, Ervid told him plainly: he was a man now, and his life was his own. Henri leveraged his considerable strength into a protection business — escorting caravans and travellers through bandit country and worse. His longest and most formative contracts sent him through the surrounding deserts, plagued with raiders and sand beasts, demanding long cold nights of unbroken vigilance. He became a sharp observer, a tireless survivor, and a very dangerous thing to ambush. What surprised him most was how much he loved it — the silence of the desert, the clarity of honest danger, the satisfaction of protecting those who couldn't protect themselves. He would have done it for free. He did not advertise this.

Ash Market & the Life Between Contracts

Between jobs, Henri lives in the Ash Market district of Aretrea — crowded, loud, and profoundly unsuited to a man of his birth. He makes peace with it because he understands the reality of earning a living, but the noise sits badly on him and the press of people fans his temper constantly. What he wants, someday, is a large house at the edge of a forest, a garden he can tend in silence, and wildlife close enough to hear at night. For now, he tolerates the market, does his work, and takes the long road home through open country whenever he can.

Current Status — The Cabbage Incident & a New Assignment
Then the contracts dried up. It was gradual at first, then sudden, and Henri suspected it had something to do with his last job: a caravan belonging to a cabbage merchant of apparently cursed disposition. Cart after cart met its end through events that were, individually, entirely ordinary — a wheel here, a runaway mule there, a gust of wind at precisely the wrong moment — but occurring with a frequency so supernatural that it defied any rational explanation. Henri watched each disaster unfold in grim silence while the merchant ran alongside his collapsing carts screaming "MY CABBAGES!" with the raw anguish of a man watching his soul leave his body in instalments. Word apparently got around.
With caravan work no longer forthcoming, Henri returned to the one man he trusted: Ervid Korr, now risen to head the Iron Hand Enforcers. Ervid, characteristically, wasted no words. He had a team that needed help — two detectives, sharp minds both, with an unfortunate and well-documented history of poor performance in close-quarters combat. They needed someone who could stand between them and whatever they kept getting themselves into. Henri accepted. It was, at minimum, quieter than the cabbage job.
